The Disaster Cycle

A recurring catastrophic cycle every ~6,000–12,000 years driven by solar activity, geomagnetic field collapse, and crustal instability.

Sources: Ben Davidson · Ethical Skeptic (ECDO) · Craig Stone · ECDO Monitor · Zacharias
1
Stage 1 — Recent history
Solar Cycle Peak & Grand Cycle
The Sun operates on an ~11-year sunspot cycle and a 22-year magnetic reversal cycle. Solar Cycle 25 has exceeded official forecasts by over 50%, producing more frequent X-class flares and CMEs than predicted. Beyond these short cycles, researchers identify a ~6,000-year and ~12,000-year grand cycle of escalating solar output correlated with geomagnetic excursion events in the paleomagnetic record. The last major event aligns with the Younger Dryas boundary (~12,800 years ago) and the Gothenburg excursion (~12,000 years ago).
SC25 50%+ above forecast~12Kyr grand cycle
2
Stage 2 — Currently underway
Geomagnetic Field Weakening
Earth’s magnetic dipole has lost ~25–35% of its strength from the Holocene peak, with the rate of decline accelerating markedly since 2000. The South Atlantic Anomaly (SAA) — a region of dangerously weak shielding — continues expanding westward and deepening. A potential North Atlantic Anomaly has also been identified. With less magnetic shielding, even moderate solar storms produce effects that would have been negligible under full field strength. The magnetospheric risk chart shows how current field loss dramatically lowers the threshold for damaging events.
~25–35% field lossSAA expanding
3
Stage 3 — Currently underway
Magnetic Pole Migration & Excursion
The magnetic north pole is migrating from Canada toward Siberia at ~55 km/year, up from ~15 km/year a century ago. This accelerating drift, combined with field weakening, signals a potential geomagnetic excursion — a temporary but dramatic weakening or partial reversal of Earth’s field. Past excursions correlate with extinction pressures, climate disruption, and elevated surface radiation. The Ethical Skeptic’s ECDO theory proposes these events involve core-mantle decoupling through a mechanism analogous to the Dzhanibekov effect — rotational instability in spinning bodies with asymmetric mass distribution.
~55 km/yr drift~12Kyr excursion cycle
4
Stage 4 — Anticipated
Grid Vulnerability & Carrington-Class Events
As the geomagnetic field weakens, the threshold for grid-damaging solar events drops dramatically. A Carrington-class CME (1859 level) under current field conditions would induce geomagnetically induced currents (GICs) strong enough to cause cascading transformer failures across continental power grids. The May 2024 G5 storm offered a preview — the strongest in 20 years. Global spare transformer inventory is critically limited, meaning widespread failure could take months to years to recover. Under continued field loss, even M-class flares begin approaching historically X-class impact levels.
1859 Carrington melted telegraph wiresLimited spare transformers
5
Stage 5 — Theoretical culmination
Solar Micronova & Magnetosphere Collapse
The proposed culminating event is a solar micronova — an extreme outburst far exceeding normal flares, observed in other Sun-like stars on cyclical timescales. Combined with a critically weakened magnetic field, a micronova would overwhelm Earth’s remaining shielding entirely. Evidence includes Aluminum-26 in Apollo lunar soil samples (short half-life isotope requiring a recent local stellar source) and ice core records showing coordinated isotope spikes at ~12,000-year intervals consistent with sudden massive solar energetic particle events. Ben Davidson’s research documents dozens of Sun-like stars producing comparable outbursts.
Micronovae observed in Sun-like starsAl-26 lunar evidence
6
Stage 6 — Theoretical (ECDO hypothesis)
Crustal Displacement & Rotational Shift
The Ethical Skeptic’s Exothermic Core-Mantle Decoupling / Dzhanibekov Oscillation (ECDO) theory proposes that core exothermic reactions, combined with magnetic field collapse and external solar energy input, trigger a physical rotation of Earth’s outer shell — an approximate 104-degree crustal displacement. Craig Stone’s paleomagnetic and geological analysis identifies physical evidence consistent with such displacements. The framework proposes Earth oscillates between two stable rotational states, with transitions producing catastrophic ocean displacement, widespread volcanism, and complete climate reorganization over a period of days to weeks.
~104° displacementDzhanibekov instability
7
Stage 7 — Aftermath & renewal
Recovery, Re-stabilization & The Next Cycle
Following displacement, Earth settles into its alternate rotational state with geographic poles in new positions. The transition involves massive ocean displacement (the primary destructive mechanism), intense volcanic and seismic activity, prolonged atmospheric disruption, and fundamental climate reorganization. Over centuries, the geomagnetic field regenerates, the Sun returns to baseline activity, ecosystems recover, and civilization can rebuild. The cycle then resets — field weakening begins again, poles resume drifting, and the clock starts on the next ~6,000–12,000-year interval. Understanding this cycle is presented as the first step toward preparation and survival.
