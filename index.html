NOAA SWPC
The Kp Index measures geomagnetic disturbance on a 0–9 scale. Higher Kp = stronger geomagnetic storm. At Kp 5+ you may see aurora at mid-latitudes. At Kp 7+ expect GPS errors, power grid stress, and HF radio blackouts.

Bz Component (IMF)
—
nT — Interplanetary Magnetic Field
NOAA SWPC
Bz is the north–south component of the interplanetary magnetic field — the “key” that unlocks Earth’s magnetosphere. When Bz goes southward (negative), it opposes Earth’s field and lets solar energy pour in — igniting geomagnetic storms.
>0 Northward0 to −5 Mild−5 to −10 Storm likely<−10 Severe

F10.7 Solar Radio Flux measures microwave radiation from the Sun — the single best proxy for overall solar activity. Higher flux = more sunspots, flares, and CME potential. Solar minimum ~70 sfu, solar max 200+.

Solar Flares — GOES X-Ray
NOAA GOES
Shows solar X-ray flux. Spikes = solar flares. M-class = moderate, X-class = major (immediate radio blackouts). Watch for sudden upward spikes.
Solar Wind — DSCOVR
Red Neg Bz = geo-effective · Purple Speed · Orange Density
NOAA DSCOVR
Multi-panel chart from DSCOVR at L1. Bz (red) negative = shields open. Density (orange) spikes = CME arrival. Speed (purple) normal ~400 km/s. This gives ~15–60 min early warning before solar wind hits Earth.
GOES Proton Flux
NOAA GOES
High-energy proton bombardment. Dotted line = S1 radiation storm. Each line up = next level. Dangerous for astronauts and satellites. If satellites are hit hard, ground effects may follow.
GOES Electron Flux
NOAA GOES
High-energy electron bombardment at geosynchronous orbit. Causes deep dielectric charging in satellites. Combined with high proton flux = significant radiation environment.
GOES Magnetometer
NOAA GOES
Magnetic field at satellite altitude. Smooth = quiet. Spikes = space weather disruption. The “canary in the coal mine” — satellites feel disruption before ground effects appear.
Geospace — 24 Hour
NOAA SWPC
Everything happening to Earth’s magnetic environment. Bz (top) negative = storms. Density spikes = CME sheath. Speed 500+ = storm. Kp 5+ = storm in progress. DST below −50 nT = genuine storm.
Aurora Forecast — Northern
NOAA SWPC
OVATION aurora model predicts aurora visibility in the next 30 min. Brighter = higher probability. During G3+ storms, the oval expands dramatically toward the equator.
ENLIL Solar Wind Spiral
NASA ISWA
WSA-ENLIL model — top-down view of the inner solar system. Earth = green dot. Orange/red blobs = CMEs propagating outward. If a blob heads directly toward the green dot, a geomagnetic storm is 1–3 days away.

The Disaster Cycle

A recurring catastrophic cycle — proposed every ~6,000 to ~12,000 years — driven by solar activity, geomagnetic field collapse, and crustal instability.

Sources: Suspicious0bservers (Ben Davidson) · The Ethical Skeptic (ECDO) · Craig Stone / Nobulart · ECDO Monitor · Zacharias
1
Stage 1 — Where we’ve been
Solar Cycle Peak & Increasing Activity
The Sun operates on an ~11-year sunspot cycle. SC25 has significantly exceeded forecasts. Researchers point to a larger ~6,000 and ~12,000-year grand cycle linked to geomagnetic excursion events.
SC25 50%+ above forecast~11yr / ~6Kyr / ~12Kyr cycles

2
Stage 2 — Currently underway
Geomagnetic Field Weakening
Earth’s magnetic dipole has been weakening at an accelerating rate — ~25–35% from its Holocene peak. The South Atlantic Anomaly continues expanding.
~25–35% field lossSAA expanding

3
Stage 3 — Currently underway
Magnetic Pole Migration & Excursion
Magnetic north migrates at ~55 km/year. The Ethical Skeptic’s ECDO theory proposes core-mantle decoupling via a Dzhanibekov effect.
~55 km/yr pole migration~12Kyr excursion cycle
Magnetic north pole — accelerating migration
4
Stage 4 — Anticipated
Grid Vulnerability & Carrington-Class Events
As field weakens, grid-damage thresholds drop. A Carrington-class event would cause cascading transformer failures. Limited spare inventory means recovery could take months to years.
1859 Carrington melted telegraph wiresLimited global spare transformers

Stage 5 — Theoretical culmination
Solar Micronova & Magnetosphere Collapse
A solar micronova — extreme outburst far exceeding normal flares. Apollo lunar soil Al-26 suggests past events. Ice cores show isotope spikes at ~12,000-year intervals.
Micronovae in Sun-like starsAl-26 lunar evidence

6
Stage 6 — Theoretical (ECDO)
Crustal Displacement & Pole Flip
The ECDO theory: core exothermic reactions + field collapse triggers an ~104-degree crustal displacement. Earth oscillates between two stable rotational states.
~104° displacementDzhanibekov instability
Dzhanibekov effect — rotational instability
7
Stage 7 — Aftermath
Recovery, Re-stabilization & The Next Cycle
Earth settles into alternate rotational state. Massive ocean displacement, volcanic/seismic activity, atmospheric disruption. Eventually field regenerates and civilization rebuilds — until the cycle repeats.
